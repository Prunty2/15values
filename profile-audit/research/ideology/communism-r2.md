# Communism revision 2: Chinese Marxist–Leninist governing tradition

Research date: 2026-10-04.

The contemporary CPC governing tradition and its socialism with Chinese characteristics, assessed through its 2022 programme and mainland Chinese laws and policy acts in force or adopted during 2022–2026. Earlier legal foundations are used where still operative. This is one expressly narrowed communist tradition, not every communist school, all Chinese citizens, or the Marx–Engels higher-phase ideal. Formal institutions are checked against governing practice; Taiwan policy is reported as the PRC’s position without endorsing its territorial claims. Unresolved questions remain unknown, not neutral. No complete placement can be published while these remain.

Coverage: 112 numerical answers; 128 unresolved. All 240 were reassessed against exact wording. No percentage has been calculated or published.

## Research method and scope change

The former higher-phase, stateless ideal could not represent incumbent communist government. All old values have therefore been discarded. The selected tradition is the CPC programme, not a claim that every communist endorses PRC acts. The 2025 private-sector law, 2024 investment list, 2023 assistance framework, 2024 BCI and genome guidance, 2025 tariff changes and 2026 monetary notice supply concrete implementation evidence. Older laws are foundations used within the assessed period.

The source set includes primary CPC and PRC legal/policy texts, independent Freedom House institutional observations, constitutional scholarship infrastructure at Constitute, and Georgetown CSET expert translation and contextualisation. SEP/IEP background distinguishes the historical ideal from the scoped governing tradition; it does not supply current policy answers. Freedom House’s scores were not imported.

## Access and evidence boundaries

Primary text was actually read through web extraction and, for the 2026 currency notice, downloaded HTML after browser extraction failed. Chinese-language legal passages were read directly. Some search-index full-text passages supplied material when direct opens failed (military Article 3, Labour Law articles 33–49, school religion policy and the 2025 Taiwan press statement); only those read passages are relied on. No access to the full permanent-residence measures was claimed. No latest tariff rate is asserted from the historical August 2025 suspension.

## Exact-question evidence decisions

### democracy-autocracy

Party monopoly excludes competitive leadership and independent opposition. Formal suffrage and congress removal powers must not be mistaken for competitive national democracy.

Counter-evidence: The constitution includes elections, recall and supervision, but independent monitoring documents Party control. Some precise institutional alternatives remain unresolved.

- **democracy-autocracy-01** (-2): National leaders should need to win regular competitive elections to remain in office.
  - National leadership is selected within Party-controlled institutions, without competitive public elections. Sources: fh2026.

- **democracy-autocracy-02** (unknown): National governments should be chosen by a panel of experts rather than by the general electorate.
  - Party selection is not identical to selection by an independent expert panel; the precise alternative is unestablished. Sources: cpc2022.

- **democracy-autocracy-03** (1): Every adult citizen should have the right to vote, regardless of their education or intelligence.
  - Article 34 excludes education-based voting restrictions, although political-rights deprivation and Party vetting limit participation. Sources: prc2018, fh2026.

- **democracy-autocracy-04** (-1): The head of government should be able to enact national laws even when parliament votes against them.
  - Articles 58 and 89 reserve statutes to the legislature; executive regulations remain legally subordinate. Sources: prc2018.

- **democracy-autocracy-05** (-2): Parties opposing the government should be allowed to campaign and stand in national elections under the same conditions as the incumbent government.
  - Opposition parties cannot compete independently with the CPC; permitted parties must accept its leadership. Sources: fh2026.

- **democracy-autocracy-06** (-2): Opposition representatives should be able to investigate the government’s use of public money.
  - Independent organised opposition is excluded, including its proposed parliamentary scrutiny role. Sources: fh2026.

- **democracy-autocracy-07** (-2): Courts independent of the government should be able to overturn government decisions that violate the constitution.
  - Party supervision of courts excludes independent constitutional checks against the governing leadership. Sources: fh2026.

- **democracy-autocracy-08** (-2): National governments should be chosen by trade union leaders rather than by the general electorate.
  - Party-led congress selection excludes substitution of an autonomous trade-union selectorate. Sources: cpc2022.

- **democracy-autocracy-09** (2): The governing leadership should have authority to exclude parties from national elections because of their political positions.
  - Maintaining CPC leadership requires exclusion of organisations challenging its governing monopoly. Sources: fh2026.

- **democracy-autocracy-10** (unknown): Voters should be able to repeal a law through a binding referendum.
  - The checked framework supplies no popular legislative-repeal referendum procedure or explicit rejection of this particular mechanism. Sources: prc2018.

- **democracy-autocracy-11** (1): National leaders should have sole authority over major national policy decisions.
  - Central Party organs determine major policy; collective organs qualify the proposition's claim of sole authority. Sources: fh2026.

- **democracy-autocracy-12** (0): Elected representatives should be able to remove the head of government for serious abuse of power through a defined constitutional process.
  - Article 63 provides legislative removal, while Party control prevents independent parliamentary enforcement: a documented institutional conflict. Sources: prc2018, fh2026.

- **democracy-autocracy-13** (-1): A national leader should be able to postpone scheduled elections without independent approval when they consider political continuity necessary.
  - Article 60 requires a two-thirds Standing Committee decision, rather than a leader's unilateral postponement. Sources: prc2018.

- **democracy-autocracy-14** (1): Voters should be able to remove an elected representative before their term ends through a defined recall vote.
  - Electoral Law articles 49–53 allow voters to petition for recall of county and township representatives, with a secret vote and majority of the original electorate required; qualified support reflects the local scope and Party-controlled institutions. Sources: electoral2020, fh2026.

- **democracy-autocracy-15** (1): National leaders should be able to govern without being accountable to voters or elected representatives.
  - Party supremacy largely displaces electoral accountability, notwithstanding formal congress supervision. Sources: fh2026, prc2018.

- **democracy-autocracy-16** (-2): The armed forces should be able to replace a civilian government on their own judgement that it is unfit to govern.
  - The Party's absolute command of the armed forces expressly rejects autonomous military replacement of civilian leadership. Sources: cpc2022.

### authority-liberty

Speech, online identity and firearms evidence supports state authority in specific areas. Detailed search, detention and personal-health thresholds remain separate questions.

Counter-evidence: Formal rights protections and civil privacy rules coexist with repression; no general authoritarian label supplies every police power.

- **authority-liberty-01** (unknown): Adults should be allowed to make personal choices such as drug use that risk personal wellbeing when they do not directly harm others.
  - The checked sources do not provide the exact personal-drug-risk prescription; general authoritarianism is insufficient. Sources: prc2018.

- **authority-liberty-02** (1): Authorities should be allowed to monitor private communications when a court authorises the surveillance to investigate serious crime.
  - Article 40 permits investigative surveillance by public-security bodies; court-authorised serious-crime surveillance is a narrower permitted inference. Sources: prc2018.

- **authority-liberty-03** (-2): Peaceful public demonstrations should be allowed even when they temporarily obstruct roads or access to businesses, provided emergency access remains available.
  - Permission requirements and suppression of peaceful protests contradict this broad protection for obstructive demonstrations. Sources: fh2026.

- **authority-liberty-04** (-2): People should be allowed to criticise public institutions, even when their criticism causes widespread offence.
  - Punishment of political criticism contradicts protection even for offensive institutional criticism. Sources: fh2026.

- **authority-liberty-05** (unknown): The government should be able to require adults to follow health measures intended primarily to protect those adults themselves.
  - The sources do not distinguish self-protective health compulsion from measures protecting other people. Sources: prc2018.

- **authority-liberty-06** (unknown): Police should be allowed to keep records of people’s movements for several years, even when those people are not suspected of an offence.
  - Mass surveillance is documented, but the checked evidence does not establish the specified multi-year retention rule. Sources: fh2026.

- **authority-liberty-07** (unknown): Police should be allowed to stop drivers at random and test them for intoxication, even without a reason to suspect impairment.
  - No checked traffic-enforcement provision establishes suspicionless intoxication testing. Sources: prc2018.

- **authority-liberty-08** (unknown): Authorities should be able to restrict a person’s movements before they commit an offence when a court finds a serious risk that they will harm others.
  - Preventive detention is documented, but the question's court-found future-harm threshold requires a distinct provision. Sources: fh2026.

- **authority-liberty-09** (-2): Adults should be allowed to access books, films, and websites that the government considers morally objectionable.
  - State censorship of online and published material rejects unrestricted adult access to prohibited content. Sources: fh2026.

- **authority-liberty-10** (-2): People should be allowed to use online communication services without having to verify their identity with the government.
  - Real-name verification and the 2025 digital identification initiative reject a general right to anonymous services. Sources: fh2026.

- **authority-liberty-11** (unknown): Police should be able to stop and search people without individual suspicion when there is a credible threat of serious violence in the area.
  - The evidence does not establish this precise threat-based suspicionless-search power. Sources: prc2018.

- **authority-liberty-12** (1): Authorities should be able to prohibit a public gathering when there is credible evidence that it is likely to lead to serious disorder.
  - The permit-and-public-order approach permits prevention of disorderly gatherings; this is narrower than documented suppression. Sources: fh2026.

- **authority-liberty-13** (-2): Police should need a court warrant to search someone’s home without their consent, except when there is an immediate threat to someone’s safety.
  - Criminal procedure regulations articles 222–224 permit searches of homes with county-level police-chief approval and a police search warrant; prior court authorisation is not required even for ordinary non-emergency searches. Sources: police2020.

- **authority-liberty-14** (-1): People should be allowed to publish confidential government information that reveals official misconduct.
  - State-secret restrictions lack the proposed general misconduct-publication exception. Sources: prc2018.

- **authority-liberty-15** (-2): Adults should be allowed to own firearms for personal defence, subject to checks for a history of violence.
  - Gun Law articles 3–6 limit possession to specified official, sporting and hunting purposes; general defensive ownership is excluded. Sources: guns.

- **authority-liberty-16** (unknown): Authorities should be allowed to hold people suspected of terrorism without independent court review until they consider the threat resolved.
  - Six-month residential surveillance is documented, but not detention lasting indefinitely until officials consider every threat resolved. Sources: fh2026.

### assimilation-multiculturalism

A shared Chinese identity is promoted alongside formal recognition of ethnic nationalities. Current assimilation practices constrain official cultural-plurality commitments.

Counter-evidence: Domestic ethnic-nationality policy is not automatically a migrant-integration policy. Funding, calendars and signage need their own evidence.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-01** (1): The government should prioritise the national culture over migrant cultures when they conflict.
  - The Party prioritises common Chinese identity and ideological cohesion when cultural commitments conflict. Sources: cpc2022.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-02** (unknown): Public services should accommodate different cultural practices, even when doing so requires additional public spending.
  - Minority recognition does not establish extra spending to accommodate the particular public-service practices contemplated. Sources: prc2018.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-03** (unknown): People who settle permanently in a country should be expected to adopt its cultural customs.
  - The checked national-identity programme does not specify migrants' obligation to adopt cultural customs. Sources: cpc2022.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-04** (unknown): Becoming part of the national community should not require giving up a foreign cultural identity.
  - Domestic ethnic protections are not automatically a rule for retaining foreign cultural identities after settlement. Sources: prc2018.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-05** (1): Public institutions should prioritise using the national language over providing services in several foreign languages.
  - The documented extension of Mandarin-medium education supports national-language priority; services beyond education require qualification. Sources: fh2026.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-06** (unknown): National ceremonies should acknowledge Indigenous peoples within the nation, even when they make up a very small share of the population.
  - Representation of recognised nationalities does not establish the proposed ceremonial recognition of Indigenous peoples. Sources: prc2018.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-07** (unknown): Government cultural funding should primarily support my country’s predominant traditions.
  - The source does not specify predominant-tradition shares of cultural grants. Sources: cpc2022.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-08** (-2): Distinct cultural identities should take priority over a shared national culture when their expectations conflict.
  - Common national unity takes precedence over separate identities under the Party programme. Sources: cpc2022.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-09** (unknown): Public institutions should organise their schedules around one common cultural calendar rather than accommodate different communities’ celebrations.
  - A shared public calendar alone would not prove that accommodation is rejected. Sources: prc2018.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-10** (1): The government should recognise several cultural traditions as part of my country’s national identity.
  - Article 4 recognises multiple nationalities and their customs, although current assimilation policies constrain that recognition. Sources: prc2018, fh2026.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-11** (unknown): Integration programmes should encourage migrants to participate in the wider national community rather than live mainly within culturally separate communities.
  - No checked migrant integration programme establishes this participation-versus-separation prescription. Sources: cpc2022.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-12** (unknown): Public signage should be written in foreign languages to accommodate migrants within their communities.
  - Minority-language protections do not establish foreign-language signage duties for migrant communities. Sources: prc2018.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-13** (1): Families should give greater emphasis to a shared national identity than to a distinct community identity.
  - The programme promotes a common Chinese national community over competing sectional identities. Sources: cpc2022.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-14** (unknown): Full body coverings, such as the burqa, should remain legal even when other full body coverings are prohibited.
  - The checked religious restrictions do not establish the exact face-covering exception posed here. Sources: fh2026.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-15** (0): My country’s national identity should include several distinct cultural ways of life.
  - Formal protection of minority customs coexists with assimilationist governance: an evidenced conflict over cultural plurality. Sources: prc2018, fh2026.

- **assimilation-multiculturalism-16** (unknown): Government integration programmes should emphasise adopting shared national customs rather than maintaining distinct community customs.
  - The sources address nationalities and citizens more directly than migrant integration-programme content. Sources: cpc2022.

### restricted-immigration-open-immigration

Residence and work rely on state permits and selected grounds. Refugee recognition, permanent settlement and ordinary labour admission are distinct.

Counter-evidence: No annual quota, cultural preference or universal sponsorship rule is inferred from selectivity alone; detailed permanent-residence regulations remain a research gap.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-01** (unknown): Permanent immigration should be subject to a strict annual numerical limit, based on housing availability and workforce deficits.
  - Selectivity is documented, but not a national annual ceiling calculated from housing and labour deficits. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-02** (-1): People should generally be allowed to settle in another country to improve their living conditions.
  - Residence requires state approval and specified grounds, rather than a general entitlement to improve living conditions. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-03** (-1): Only applicants with skills considered important to my country should normally qualify for permanent residence.
  - The statute allows humanitarian grounds and other residence categories beyond economically important skills. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-04** (unknown): Partners and dependent children of immigrants who are permanently settled in my country should generally be allowed to join them.
  - The checked entry law does not settle the scope of permanent residents' family-reunification entitlement. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-05** (-1): Refugees fleeing their home countries should be allowed to settle permanently, regardless of their skills or cultural compatibility.
  - The framework distinguishes recognised refugee residence from permanent residence, which requires separate approval. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-06** (-1): People who migrate illegally should be allowed to remain in my country rather than face deportation.
  - The law requires departure when lawful stay expires and penalises unlawful residence; no general right to remain follows. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-07** (1): Permanent immigration should be limited even when employers want to recruit more workers from abroad.
  - Articles 41–42 require permits and state-defined employment needs beyond an employer's wish to recruit. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-08** (unknown): Immigration should be reduced to ease pressure on housing and public services, even when additional arrivals would bring economic benefits.
  - No checked provision gives housing and service pressure priority over immigration's economic benefits. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-09** (unknown): Family immigration should count towards the same annual numerical limit as other permanent immigration.
  - Neither a shared family quota nor the relevant general annual ceiling is established. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-10** (-1): People with a confirmed job offer should generally be allowed to move to my country for work.
  - A job offer alone does not replace the work-permit and residence-permit requirements. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-11** (unknown): Applicants for permanent settlement should need sponsorship from an employer or family member.
  - The statute refers permanent-residence criteria to further rules; universal sponsorship is not established. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-12** (unknown): Graduating from one of my country’s universities should not, by itself, provide a route to permanent residence.
  - Graduation is not an automatic permanent status in the checked statute, but whether it alone supplies a route requires detailed admission regulations. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-13** (unknown): People fleeing persecution should be allowed to enter and receive protection even when ordinary immigration limits have been reached.
  - Article 46 provides refugee screening, but does not address admission after ordinary numerical limits are exhausted. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-14** (unknown): The government should prioritise migrants from culturally similar countries.
  - The checked criteria do not establish cultural similarity as an immigration preference. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-15** (unknown): My country should allow non-citizen migrants to receive welfare benefits or live in social housing.
  - Protection of foreigners' lawful interests does not specify cash-welfare or social-housing eligibility. Sources: entrylaw.

- **restricted-immigration-open-immigration-16** (unknown): My country should allow more migrants from diverse backgrounds because their cultures differ from our own.
  - Economic opening does not establish a cultural-diversity rationale for increasing immigration. Sources: cpc2022.

### militarist-pacifist

The scoped tradition promotes national defence and peaceful diplomacy while retaining coercive options over Taiwan. This is not absolute pacifism.

Counter-evidence: Universal conscription, autonomous weapons and overseas campaigns require specific evidence; a military modernisation objective does not settle all these questions.

- **militarist-pacifist-01** (1): My country should be willing to use military force to secure national interests, even when it has not been attacked.
  - The 2025 Taiwan statement retains force as an option beyond responding to an armed attack. Sources: taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-02** (1): Diplomatic measures should take priority over military action, even when they are slower to achieve my country’s aims.
  - The same statement prioritises peaceful reunification while retaining military options. Sources: taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-03** (unknown): My country should be willing to bear substantial financial costs to maintain military superiority over potential opponents.
  - World-class military modernisation does not establish willingness to bear the specified substantial financial cost specifically for superiority over opponents. Sources: cpc2022.

- **militarist-pacifist-04** (unknown): Military action should be permitted to prevent an imminent attack when there is strong evidence that the attack is being prepared.
  - The checked statements do not establish the exact imminent-attack evidence threshold for pre-emption. Sources: taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-05** (-1): My country should use armed force only to respond to an armed attack against itself or an ally.
  - Retaining force for reunification rejects an exclusively attack-response doctrine. Sources: taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-06** (unknown): My country should require male citizens to complete a period of military service to train a large, capable force and deter potential aggressors.
  - Articles 3 and 19–21 combine a voluntary mainstay with selective compulsory male enlistment. This supports a male service duty, but does not settle the question if understood as general compulsory completion by male citizens. Sources: service2021.

- **militarist-pacifist-07** (-1): My country should accept substantial diplomatic concessions to prevent a war, even when doing so weakens its international position.
  - The stated reunification commitment limits willingness to surrender core territorial aims solely to prevent conflict. Sources: taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-08** (unknown): My country should develop AI-powered weapons that can select and attack targets without a human approving each attack if this would make military operations more efficient and improve national security.
  - No checked weapon policy establishes autonomous target engagement without human approval. Sources: cpc2022.

- **militarist-pacifist-09** (1): Defence policy should focus on repelling attacks rather than conducting military campaigns abroad.
  - The constitution assigns the armed forces defence and resistance to aggression, qualified by territorial force options. Sources: prc2018, taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-10** (-2): My country should avoid war and military action at all costs, even if an ally is being attacked.
  - The retained military option rejects avoiding force under every circumstance. Sources: taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-11** (unknown): My country should maintain forces capable of prolonged combat beyond its borders.
  - World-class-force aspirations alone do not establish an explicit prolonged overseas-combat prescription. Sources: cpc2022.

- **militarist-pacifist-12** (-1): My country should leave a territorial dispute unresolved rather than start a war to settle it.
  - The 2025 policy retains force rather than committing to leave the Taiwan dispute indefinitely unresolved. Sources: taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-13** (1): My country should be willing to threaten military action to obtain concessions in serious international disputes.
  - An expressly retained force option is used to deter separatist outcomes and external intervention. Sources: taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-14** (0): When the two conflict, reducing the risk of war should take priority over demonstrating military resolve.
  - Peaceful reunification is preferred, but military resolve is explicitly retained; neither priority is unconditional. Sources: taiwan2025.

- **militarist-pacifist-15** (unknown): My country should use military espionage to gain an advantage over potential opponents, even when doing so risks worsening relations with them.
  - No checked doctrine establishes the exact espionage-versus-diplomatic-cost tradeoff. Sources: cpc2022.

- **militarist-pacifist-16** (unknown): My country should accept reductions in its military capabilities as part of negotiated efforts to prevent war.
  - Peaceful-development language does not specify accepting negotiated military-capability reductions. Sources: cpc2022.

### nationalism-internationalism

Sovereignty and noninterference strongly constrain international authority, while negotiated cooperation and development projects are supported.

Counter-evidence: Treaty cooperation is distinct from supranational jurisdiction. Monetary pooling and particular judicial conflicts remain unresolved.

- **nationalism-internationalism-01** (2): My country’s institutions should have final authority over laws that apply within its borders.
  - The Party's sovereignty and noninterference principles favour final domestic authority. Sources: cpc2022.

- **nationalism-internationalism-02** (-1): International institutions should have authority to make binding decisions for my country, even when my government opposes those decisions.
  - Sovereignty-centred cooperation rejects unrestricted external decisions over national objections. Sources: cpc2022.

- **nationalism-internationalism-03** (unknown): Courts in my country should have final authority when national law conflicts with an international court’s ruling.
  - Sovereignty commitments do not specify the exact domestic-versus-international judicial conflict rule. Sources: cpc2022.

- **nationalism-internationalism-04** (unknown): My country should make long-term commitments to international alliances, even when those commitments limit its freedom to act independently.
  - International cooperation is established; alliances constraining independent action need more specific evidence. Sources: cpc2022.

- **nationalism-internationalism-05** (1): My country should be able to withdraw from international commitments without needing approval from international institutions.
  - Constitution article 81 places treaty abrogation within domestic institutions, supporting domestic withdrawal authority. Sources: prc2018.

- **nationalism-internationalism-06** (unknown): International courts should have binding authority over disputes involving my government, including cases brought against its own officials.
  - The sources do not establish acceptance or rejection of every international court's jurisdiction over officials. Sources: cpc2022.

- **nationalism-internationalism-07** (1): My country should prioritise its own interests when they conflict with goals set by international institutions.
  - Safeguarding sovereignty and development interests qualifies external collective goals. Sources: cpc2022.
