## Scope and interpretation

The scope is parliamentary mixed-economy social democracy in Socialist International principles, not every party calling itself social democratic.

Reformist parliamentary social democracy, combining a mixed economy, universal social provision and labour rights. The Socialist International declaration includes democratic-socialist aspirations; this profile selects its explicit mixed-economy formulation rather than assuming total social ownership. This is a sourced interpretation of the specified tradition, not an adherent questionnaire. Unresolved evidence or an unrepresented institutional premise is recorded as unknown, never as numerical neutrality. Historical sources are not treated as answers to modern technologies by analogy. Government acts are contextual evidence only, not a substitute for the scoped doctrine.

## Contemporary acts

The UK government statement published 1 June 2026 confirms GTR operating services transferred on 31 May. This is ownership of the operating company/service, not a transfer of passenger rail infrastructure, and does not prove an all-sector nationalisation doctrine.

## Counter-evidence and remaining research limits

The declaration deliberately allows different public/private mixes: this supports genuine conditional positions where questions ask for a universal ownership presumption. It does not establish each specific sector’s required ownership. The exact empirical causal rankings and many policing, immigration and constitutional procedures remain unprescribed.

Research looked for doctrinal positions rather than contemporary scientific answers to the Culture vs Nature questions. A source’s moral equality claim is not treated as empirical evidence. No score, similarity target or expected ideological location was used. Neutral answers below are explicit mixed or conditional judgements, never placeholders for unknown evidence.

## Changes supported by the new readings

- `innovation-caution-05`: None → 0. The Tirana declaration embraces useful AI but requires protection of displaced workers through retraining, preserved employment or fair compensation and corporate contributions. Productivity alone is insufficient: the stated support is explicitly conditional on managing displacement.
- `innovation-caution-13`: None → -2. Paragraph 53 of the declaration expressly calls for preventing manipulation of human genetic material. Controlled enhancement trials fall within that objection; this is the declaration’s position, not a claim about the scientific safety of gene editing.
- `public-private-04`: None → 1. Paragraphs 59–62 retain public enterprises where social goals require them within a mixed economy. They reject treating competitive production as an exclusively private domain, without advocating public firms in every industry.
- `public-private-01`: None → 0. The declaration deliberately rejects a universal ownership formula: both private and public enterprises can serve social goals, and their mix depends on conditions (paragraphs 59–62). It therefore prescribes neither a private majority nor its opposite as a universal rule.
- `public-private-09`: None → -1. The declaration evaluates enterprises by democratic social objectives rather than treating private ownership as intrinsically superior (paragraphs 59–62). This opposes a general privatisation instruction while permitting justified individual sales.
- `public-private-15`: None → 0. The declaration’s mixed-economy test considers social purpose as well as efficiency, and rejects a single ownership solution (paragraphs 59–62). Repeated subsidy neither automatically warrants privatisation nor guarantees retention; the actual policy is conditional on wider public benefit.
