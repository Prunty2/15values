## Counter-evidence and remaining research limits

The inheritance speech supports progressive legacy duties in transition; it does not establish every modern cash-benefit design. Engels’s family history supports social explanations of gendered conduct but includes hostile same-sex judgements, so it cannot prove modern marriage/adoption positions. Detailed genetic-causation and modern technology thresholds remain genuine historical gaps.

Research looked for doctrinal positions rather than contemporary scientific answers to the Culture vs Nature questions. A source’s moral equality claim is not treated as empirical evidence. No score, similarity target or expected ideological location was used. Neutral answers below are explicit mixed or conditional judgements, never placeholders for unknown evidence.

## Changes supported by the new readings

- `high-redistribution-low-redistribution-07`: None → 1. Marx’s 1869 speech advocates progressive legacy duties, exempting small amounts, and links reform to securing subsistence and transforming social conditions. This supports inheritance-tax-funded support in transition, although it does not set a modern earmarked cash-benefit formula.
- `culture-nature-05`: None → 1. The 1844 manuscripts explain cultivated interests such as musical appreciation through historical development and the opportunities material conditions permit. This supports social experience as a major determinant of differing interests, while acknowledging bodily senses rather than denying inherited capacities.
- `culture-nature-03`: None → 1. Engels compares women’s conduct and development under different property, education and family systems and explicitly locates patriarchal monogamy in economic conditions. This supports a qualified social explanation of observed gendered behaviour; it does not assert that all sex differences are culturally produced.
- `progressive-traditionalist-04`: -1 → -1. Engels treats male supremacy and women’s confinement to domestic service as products of property relations to be overcome. That opposes preserving those traditional role expectations merely because they are customary, while leaving room for freely chosen differences.
