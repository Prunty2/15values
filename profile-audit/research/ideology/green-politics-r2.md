## Counter-evidence and remaining research limits

General precaution supplies modest, labelled inferences on novel medical and enhancement technologies without pretending the Charter names them. It does not supply a position on every ownership sector or comparative genetic explanation. Anti-discrimination and biodiversity commitments are not substitutes for human-behaviour causal evidence.

Research looked for doctrinal positions rather than contemporary scientific answers to the Culture vs Nature questions. A source’s moral equality claim is not treated as empirical evidence. No score, similarity target or expected ideological location was used. Neutral answers below are explicit mixed or conditional judgements, never placeholders for unknown evidence.

## Changes supported by the new readings

- `innovation-caution-04`: None → 1. The Charter’s precautionary principle and paragraph 7.10 apply ethical and precautionary safeguards to scientific research generally. Applied to widespread medical use, they support studying long-term safety before release; this is a modest normative application, not a claimed medical-technology-specific resolution.
- `innovation-caution-07`: None → -1. The Charter’s ecological precaution requires avoiding poorly understood environmental harm. Applied to replacing conventional production with cultivated meat, it opposes early widespread replacement before environmental effects are understood, without rejecting eventual safe use.
- `innovation-caution-08`: None → 1. Applying the Charter’s precautionary treatment of scientific research to elective brain enhancement supports research-only use until stronger long-term safety evidence. The Charter does not name brain implants, so the answer is an explicitly limited inference.
- `innovation-caution-11`: None → -1. Basic safety alone does not meet the Charter’s precautionary standard when long-term irreversible effects remain uncertain. This applies that general principle to elective implants rather than attributing a technology-specific ban to the Charter.
- `innovation-caution-12`: None → 1. The Charter combines precaution in research with social justice. For medical AI, that supports supervised evaluation until reliability across patient groups is established; this is an application of stated principles, not an assertion that the Charter contains an AI clause.
- `militarist-pacifist-05`: None → -1. The Charter permits force as a last resort under a UN mandate to prevent mass atrocities. That exception goes beyond responding to an attack on the country or its ally, while remaining much narrower than routine interventionism.
