## Scope and interpretation

The programme was narrowed to the German CDU social-market tradition, whose 2024 text is sufficiently specific to distinguish it from Catholic doctrine and other EPP parties. Its explicit same-sex family recognition replaces generic traditionalist assumptions.

German CDU Christian democracy as specified by its May 2024 fundamental programme, situated within the European personalist and social-market tradition. The programme is the primary source for current applications; EPP principles and Catholic social teaching explain its intellectual setting but do not override explicit CDU positions. Incumbent government acts are dated corroboration or counter-evidence, not automatic replacements for party doctrine. This is a sourced interpretation, not an adherent questionnaire. Unknown means the exact proposition is not established; neither equality nor social policy alone establishes a comparative biological-causation belief.

## Counter-evidence and remaining research limits

The programme has a Christian account of social nature and individual differences, but does not rank genetic and environmental causes for country IQ, stress, attention, temperament, attractiveness or racial violence. Personal dignity is not evidence answering those questions. It also leaves many precise policing procedures and biomedical thresholds unsettled.

Research looked for doctrinal positions rather than contemporary scientific answers to the Culture vs Nature questions. A source’s moral equality claim is not treated as empirical evidence. No score, similarity target or expected ideological location was used. Neutral answers below are explicit mixed or conditional judgements, never placeholders for unknown evidence.

## Changes supported by the new readings

- `authority-liberty-01`: None → -2. The programme explicitly rejects drug legalisation and combines prevention, treatment, harm reduction and repression (printed p.60); its objection extends beyond drugs directly harming other people.
- `authority-liberty-02`: None → 1. The programme supports limited retention of connection data and judicially authorised access to investigate serious crime and terrorism (pp.18–19). This supports the safeguarded surveillance proposition, without authorising unrestricted monitoring.
- `authority-liberty-06`: None → -1. The programme requires data retention to be limited to the necessary minimum and connects access to serious-crime investigation; a routine multi-year movement record of unsuspected people goes beyond that limitation.
- `assimilation-multiculturalism-01`: None → 1. The programme makes a shared Leitkultur, including language, history and customs, the framework for integration (pp.32–33), while retaining constitutional pluralism. It therefore gives the common culture qualified priority in conflicts.
- `assimilation-multiculturalism-03`: None → 1. Integration includes acquiring German language and familiarity with the country’s culture and history, as well as accepting constitutional norms (pp.32–39). This supports an expectation of adopting shared customs without requiring complete cultural erasure.
- `assimilation-multiculturalism-05`: None → 1. German language is treated as the central means of successful integration and public participation; the programme also calls for German-language religious training. This supports a national-language priority, not a ban on necessary translation.
- `assimilation-multiculturalism-13`: None → 1. Leitkultur and transmission of shared cultural roots are presented as prerequisites of cohesion. Applying that family-and-community duty supports emphasis on shared identity while retaining personal heritage.
- `assimilation-multiculturalism-16`: None → 1. The programme’s integration model requires participation in a shared language, history and culture; preserving separate community customs is not its organising priority.
- `restricted-immigration-open-immigration-02`: None → -1. The asylum chapter distinguishes protection from economic migration and denies a protection entitlement based on poor economic conditions alone (p.24). Admission is controlled rather than a general entitlement to settle for improved living conditions.
- `restricted-immigration-open-immigration-05`: None → -1. The programme favours protection near countries of origin, safe-third-country procedures and limited humanitarian admission with integration and security criteria (pp.23–24). It does not grant every refugee unconditional permanent settlement.
- `restricted-immigration-open-immigration-06`: None → -2. The programme rejects remaining in Europe under an asylum pretext without a protection entitlement and demands restored control of irregular migration (pp.23–24).
- `restricted-immigration-open-immigration-08`: None → 1. Humanitarian migration is to be limited to a level within Germany’s capacity; the programme links migration control to the ability to integrate and provide services. This supports a qualified capacity limit despite economic gains, not a quantified annual ceiling.
- `restricted-immigration-open-immigration-13`: 2 → 0. The programme preserves constitutional protection for qualifying directly arriving persecuted people while proposing third-country processing and controlled humanitarian contingents (pp.23–24). These explicit commitments make entry beyond ordinary limits conditional, rather than categorically open or closed.
- `militarist-pacifist-01`: None → 1. The programme explicitly allows military protection of international trade routes (p.51), beyond responding to an attack on German territory. This supports limited national-interest uses, constrained by international law and alliances.
- `militarist-pacifist-06`: None → 1. The programme proposes reversing the suspension of conscription and an interim contingent military draft (p.27). Its eventual universal social-service year qualifies agreement with military service specifically.
- `militarist-pacifist-11`: None → 1. The programme demands capable, sustained-combat armed forces, NATO collective defence and independent European capacity to act (pp.27–28). These commitments support sustained operations beyond Germany, without treating every foreign campaign as justified.
- `nationalism-internationalism-13`: None → -2. Frontex is to become a genuine border and coast guard with sovereign powers, and European external borders are to be protected jointly (pp.22–23). This directly rejects sole national border authority.
- `nationalism-internationalism-11`: None → -2. The programme endorses the euro and independent European Central Bank while rejecting debt mutualisation (pp.30–31). National ownership of monetary policy is not its chosen model.
- `public-private-03`: 1 → 2. The programme states private provision should precede state provision, with government acting where private actors cannot perform as well or better (pp.72,78). Comparable outcomes therefore favour private ownership.
- `public-private-07`: None → 1. The general private-before-state rule applies to public tasks when private providers can perform them as well or better (p.78). Comparable access and quality at lower cost meet that condition; public responsibility for standards remains.
- `public-private-09`: None → 1. Applying the programme’s explicit subsidiary role for state enterprise supports selling ordinary commercial businesses where no special public function requires ownership. This is a qualified inference, not an instruction to privatise every strategic asset.
- `public-private-13`: None → 1. Routine service provision falls under the programme’s private-before-state presumption, subject to adequate performance (p.78). That principle supports contracting suitable routine services.
- `public-private-16`: None → 2. The programme explicitly retains Germany’s dual system of public-service and private broadcasting (pp.40–41), rejecting an entirely privately owned broadcasting system.
- `protectionism-free-trade-06`: None → -1. The programme says trade policy rests on reciprocity and strategic alliances (p.51). It supports liberalisation through agreements rather than a general duty to remove barriers unilaterally.
- `protectionism-free-trade-09`: None → -1. The programme explicitly supports free and fair agricultural trade (p.68), pairing it with domestic agricultural policy. Preserving farming does not by itself establish a preference for import restrictions.
- `protectionism-free-trade-15`: None → 1. The programme proposes protecting critical infrastructure and companies against takeovers by systemic rivals (p.52). The support is qualified because it does not exclude every foreign strategic investor.
- `planning-free-market-14`: None → -2. The programme expressly protects central-bank independence and rejects monetary state financing (pp.30–31). Directing lending and interest rates through a government economic plan contradicts this rule.
- `high-redistribution-low-redistribution-09`: None → -2. The programme explicitly rejects reinstating a wealth tax (p.55). A transfer-spending purpose does not remove that stated objection.
- `secular-religious-10`: None → 1. The programme supports Islamic theology and German-language imam training at universities as well as public cooperation with religious communities (pp.37–38). These are qualified forms of public support for religious life, not a commitment to finance every proselytising activity.
- `secular-religious-11`: None → -1. The programme protects Christian symbols in public space while affirming religious freedom and state neutrality (p.37). Applied to government display, this opposes a categorical removal rule, with pluralist qualifications.
- `secular-religious-12`: None → 2. The programme explicitly preserves Christian holidays and Sunday protection as part of the country’s shared religious-cultural heritage (p.37).
- `progressive-traditionalist-03`: None → 1. The programme’s concept of family expressly includes same-sex marriages, single parents and blended families (p.34). It recognises their care responsibilities while still giving marriage a special place.
- `progressive-traditionalist-05`: None → 2. Marriage is defined as a lasting union of two people and same-sex marriages are expressly included in its family model (p.34).
- `progressive-traditionalist-06`: 1 → 0. The programme favours lasting marriage and family stability but explicitly recognises same-sex marriages, single-parent and blended families. Its preferred stable-care model does not unconditionally rank every two-parent married household above other families.
- `progressive-traditionalist-07`: None → -1. The programme maintains the existing abortion compromise and seeks to encourage carrying pregnancies to term while supporting women (p.35). It therefore does not endorse uncomplicated social acceptance, but neither does it demand an absolute prohibition.
- `progressive-traditionalist-13`: None → 1. Women and men should have equal opportunities across work, leadership and family responsibilities under the programme (pp.35–36). That supports acceptance of crossing customary roles even though the programme retains a binary account of biological sex.
- `innovation-caution-09`: None → 1. The programme prioritises technological climate solutions including hydrogen, advanced nuclear power and fusion, alongside efficiency (pp.63–64). This supports a technology-first approach with some unproven technologies, rather than an exclusive commitment to consumption reduction.
- `central-local-06`: None → -2. The programme retains the federal-state division of police responsibilities while strengthening national information coordination (p.20). Coordination is not a single national police command.
- `central-local-09`: None → 2. The federalism section explicitly calls for greater local tax autonomy and strong self-governing municipalities (p.75), accepting differentiated local tax decisions.
