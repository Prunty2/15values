# Civic nationalism (Scottish SNP tradition): revision 2 research dossier

Research resumed 2026-10-04. Incomplete research draft; no archive or percentage publication authorised by this dossier.

Scope: The SNP’s civic and social-democratic Scottish national project, anchored in its 2026 manifesto and checked against the Scottish Government’s 2026–27 budget and independent research on national identity. Scotland means the proposed self-governing nation in national questions; regions means its local territories except the explicitly applicable secession principle. Government delivery is evidence of this variant, not the definition of all civic nationalism. Manifesto intentions and budget allocations are distinguished from completed delivery; the cited budget is evidence of government spending plans, not proof that every allocation has been spent. Strict revision-2 re-audit: assess only source-supported positions on the exact questions. Unsupported empirical comparisons, modern technology analogies and institutional particulars remain unknown. Absence of doctrine is not a neutral position. This draft must not be used to calculate or publish a complete profile. Scottish independence preferences are not automatically transferred to the power of every sub-Scottish region or municipality.

Current coverage: 97 numerical answers and 143 unresolved answers. All 240 exact question records remain in the draft.

## Evidence checked and limitations

The scope remains the SNP’s specified contemporary civic-nationalist programme, not every form of civic nationalism. The manifesto and Scottish budget evidence are distinguished from delivered government outcomes. Scottish Government trauma policy was investigated for an acquired-development account.

Trauma-informed interventions establish that experience matters; they do not establish that it explains more stress sensitivity than biology. The Scottish Government page is dated 29 July 2026 and describes April 2025 funding; those dates must not be conflated. National identity scholarship does not provide a complete behavioural-science doctrine.
