## Axis-specific counter-evidence

### democracy-autocracy

### authority-liberty

### assimilation-multiculturalism

Venetian civic identity accommodated several trading and subject communities, but Jews lived under ghetto restrictions and distinct communities lacked equal political status.

### restricted-immigration-open-immigration

The commercial city received foreign merchants and workers under regulated status, without an equal unconditional residence entitlement; historical passports differ from modern numerical immigration ceilings.

Separate immigration from emigration, colonial occupation and coerced labour. Modern numerical quotas, welfare entitlements and graduate routes require analogy to actual admission practices.  historical passports differ from modern numerical immigration ceilings.

### militarist-pacifist

After the 1718 settlement Venice mostly pursued neutrality and avoided major wars, though it maintained forces and colonial possessions.

### nationalism-internationalism

The republic defended independence and diplomatic neutrality rather than submitting its institutions to an international political authority.

### public-private

Measure legal or social ownership, not whether private firms were regulated. Modern service-contracting and broadcasting questions require comparison with period public services; social ownership is not ordinary private capital.  guild organisation was not general state ownership.

### protectionism-free-trade

Commercial openness and maritime exchange coexisted with monopolies, guild protection and protective duties, especially under relative economic decline.

### planning-free-market

Guild regulation and public commercial institutions shaped production without a modern comprehensive national output plan.

### high-redistribution-low-redistribution

Public services and spending do not establish equalising cash transfers. Compare eligible groups and actual taxation, relief, inheritance and land policy; compensation and modern welfare language may require approximation. Charity, confraternities and public assistance supported poor residents without systematically equalising patrician wealth or landholding.

### secular-religious

Civilian policy power, religious schooling, holidays, marriage and clerical offices can point in different directions. Catholicism was official, but Venice defended state jurisdiction against papal interference and regulated ecclesiastical power.

### progressive-traditionalist

Patrician privilege, marriage and Christian moral institutions remained traditional despite the city's cosmopolitan and theatrical culture.

### innovation-caution

Separate material development from an unrestricted willingness to accept irreversible harms. AI, cultivated meat, implants, climate technology and embryo editing are explicitly historical analogies, not documented period proposals.  modern biomedical choices are especially uncertain.

### central-local

Distinguish formal constitutional autonomy, local administration and effective party or imperial control. Customary institutions are not necessarily protected self-government; contemporary treatment and tax mechanisms require analogy.  local autonomy did not imply a right to secede.

### culture-nature

This axis concerns causes of traits, not environmental policy or whether groups received equal rights.
