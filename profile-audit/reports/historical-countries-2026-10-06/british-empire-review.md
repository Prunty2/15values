## Axis-specific counter-evidence

### democracy-autocracy

British parliamentary competition and expanding metropolitan suffrage did not give most colonial subjects equivalent control; governors and imperial officials held executive authority abroad.

Assess access to effective governing power, including excluded populations; plebiscites and nominal constitutional rights are not equivalent to competitive control. Representative government does not establish support for binding direct votes.  governors and imperial officials held executive authority abroad.

### authority-liberty

Metropolitan civil liberties contrasted with colonial emergency powers, censorship and coercive policing, including repression of Indian protest.

Keep ordinary reviewed health, security and policing restrictions distinct from arbitrary political repression. Unequal protection across classes or colonies qualifies generalisation. Online communications are an analogy to period communications. Metropolitan civil liberties contrasted with colonial emergency powers, censorship and coercive policing, including repression of Indian protest.

### assimilation-multiculturalism

Imperial rule combined promotion of British institutions with indirect rule, recognised communal law and separate cultural administration rather than equal citizenship everywhere.

Distinguish coercive exclusion from assimilation and administrative recognition from equal membership. Apply migrant wording by analogy where the evidence concerns established minorities, with reduced confidence. Imperial rule combined promotion of British institutions with indirect rule, recognised communal law and separate cultural administration rather than equal citizenship everywhere.

### restricted-immigration-open-immigration

Migration rules varied by dominion and colony; racial exclusion and selective settler movement outweighed any general equal right of imperial subjects to settle throughout the empire.

Separate immigration from emigration, colonial occupation and coerced labour. Modern numerical quotas, welfare entitlements and graduate routes require analogy to actual admission practices.  racial exclusion and selective settler movement outweighed any general equal right of imperial subjects to settle throughout the empire.

### militarist-pacifist

A worldwide navy, garrisons and colonial campaigns enforced imperial interests, alongside interwar disarmament and diplomacy.

Separate defensive capacity, actual military deployment and diplomatic restraint. Autonomous AI weapons did not exist; that answer is an uncertain analogy to willingness to delegate military technology and use force. A worldwide navy, garrisons and colonial campaigns enforced imperial interests, alongside interwar disarmament and diplomacy.

### nationalism-internationalism

Britain supported the League of Nations and treaties while retaining imperial sovereignty and resisting outside control over colonial government.

Treat cooperation as binding shared authority only where institutions actually constrained the state. Contemporary climate, human-rights and monetary bodies require institutional analogy. Britain supported the League of Nations and treaties while retaining imperial sovereignty and resisting outside control over colonial government.

### public-private

Private enterprise and capital remained central, with public railways, utilities and the BBC; colonial ownership varied by territory and sector.

Measure legal or social ownership, not whether private firms were regulated. Modern service-contracting and broadcasting questions require comparison with period public services; social ownership is not ordinary private capital.  colonial ownership varied by territory and sector.

### protectionism-free-trade

The interwar period shifted from substantial free-trade traditions to imperial preference, particularly the 1932 Ottawa agreements; intra-imperial preference excluded equal treatment of outsiders.

Do not confuse export activity with open imports or forced sanctions with a voluntary policy. Online-service trade is analogous to cross-border commercial access, not a historical digital policy.  intra-imperial preference excluded equal treatment of outsiders.

### planning-free-market

Markets organised most production, supplemented by imperial development policy, monetary management and sectoral controls; there was no economy-wide compulsory plan.

Measure production, price, investment and wage direction separately from ownership. Private currency and central-bank mechanisms require period institutional comparison; absence of a modern institution is not an automatic answer.  there was no economy-wide compulsory plan.

### high-redistribution-low-redistribution

British insurance, progressive taxation and unemployment assistance expanded, while colonial budgets offered much less redistributive coverage and preserved large asset inequalities.

Public services and spending do not establish equalising cash transfers. Compare eligible groups and actual taxation, relief, inheritance and land policy; compensation and modern welfare language may require approximation. British insurance, progressive taxation and unemployment assistance expanded, while colonial budgets offered much less redistributive coverage and preserved large asset inequalities.

### secular-religious

The Crown, established churches and official Christian ceremonies had political roles, while parliamentary legislation and colonial recognition of several religious legal systems limited clerical supremacy.

Measure religion in state law and institutions rather than population belief or a leader’s private faith. Civilian policy power, religious schooling, holidays, marriage and clerical offices can point in different directions. The Crown, established churches and official Christian ceremonies had political roles, while parliamentary legislation and colonial recognition of several religious legal systems limited clerical supremacy.

### progressive-traditionalist

Reform in Britain occurred alongside traditional marriage, gender and sexual norms; metropolitan social change was not equal protection throughout colonial society.

Distinguish gender equality and selected reforms from present-day recognition of same-sex marriage or gender identity. Apply family and sexual norms separately; parents’ control may conflict with state-directed schooling.  metropolitan social change was not equal protection throughout colonial society.

### innovation-caution

Imperial railways, mining, industrial science and communications promoted development, but conservation and medical safeguards also existed; futuristic technologies require analogy.

Separate material development from an unrestricted willingness to accept irreversible harms. AI, cultivated meat, implants, climate technology and embryo editing are explicitly historical analogies, not documented period proposals.  futuristic technologies require analogy.

### central-local

Dominion autonomy grew under the 1931 Statute of Westminster; colonies remained subject to imperial oversight, often through local rulers and administrators.

Distinguish formal constitutional autonomy, local administration and effective party or imperial control. Customary institutions are not necessarily protected self-government; contemporary treatment and tax mechanisms require analogy.  colonies remained subject to imperial oversight, often through local rulers and administrators.

### culture-nature

Colonial racial classifications and contemporary hereditary theories coexisted with civilising and educational doctrines; neither can establish a single empirical theory shared by the empire.

This axis concerns causes of traits, not environmental policy or whether groups received equal rights. Education and moral cultivation provide limited evidence for learned formation; hierarchy does not prove a genetic claim about every trait. Specific modern IQ, stress, empathy and attention hypotheses remain low-confidence analogies.  neither can establish a single empirical theory shared by the empire.
