# Historical country religious-population review

Supplement revision 2 preserves every revision-1 entry. Five historical subjects have a within-period numerical Christian population share: Nazi Germany (1939, 94%), Weimar Germany (1925, 96.5%), Venice (1770, 99.8%), East Germany (1989, approximately 30%), German Empire (1910, 98.74%). Population affiliation is independent of governing ideology or religious law. The German census source is the German Historical Institute; Venice and East Germany use explicitly cited historical secondary summaries.

For the other fifteen subjects, the consulted sources did not establish a numerical share for the exact period and whole governed-population scope. These entries deliberately record missing evidence, not an assumed zero population. Their sourceYear 2026 is the review cutoff, explicitly identified in each rationale; no modern census is applied to a historical state. They remain eligible for general ideologies and excluded from faith-specific comparisons in both directions under the existing rule. This is a limitation for subsequent evidence enrichment, not a null scored-answer placeholder.

The validator accepts positive historical census years to support Venice’s 1770 evidence; the 20% threshold, identity exclusions and sourced-share requirements are unchanged. A regression check verifies pre-1900 evidence, threshold rejection and invalid-year rejection.

Nazi Germany’s GHI reference uses 1937 borders and records 60.8% Protestant plus 33.2% Catholic; the summed 94% is retained with that territorial limitation. German Empire shares were calculated from the primary table per 10,000 residents: (6,159 + 3,669 + 46) / 100 = 98.74%.
