## Evidence and qualifications

### Rhodesia

1965–May 1979. The Rhodesian Front defended white-minority political control and settler sovereignty through racially restricted parliamentary institutions. Authoritarian capitalism is a broad economic comparison, not the complete historical tradition.

- [Possible Speeding up of Southern Rhodesian UDI](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v24/d466) · US Department of State
- [Land Inequality and Income Distribution in Rhodesia](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/african-studies-review/article/abs/land-inequality-and-income-distribution-in-rhodesia/28114E92CFABC4F266675EEF8B922049) · Cambridge University Press

### British Empire

1919–1939. Interwar parliamentary government in Britain coexisted with colonial administration, racial inequality and growing dominion autonomy. Militarism describes one policy dimension rather than the empire’s whole political tradition.

- [Colonial Office records and colonial administration](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/on-the-record-bonus-colonial-office-records.pdf) · The National Archives
- [Britain’s Empire Marketing Board and the failure of soft trade policy, 1926–33](https://academic.oup.com/ereh/article/25/4/780/6206535) · Oxford University Press

### Nazi Germany

1933–1945. National Socialism was the governing party ideology: racial hierarchy, antisemitism, dictatorship and expansionism. This classification is directly historical, distinct from a numerical resemblance.

- [Foundations of the Nazi State](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/foundations-of-the-nazi-state) · United States Holocaust Memorial Museum
- [NS-Regime: Industrie und Wirtschaft](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/ns-regime/wirtschaft) · Deutsches Historisches Museum

### Soviet Union

1928–1953. The selected 1928–1953 period concerns Stalinist party rule, central planning and the official Marxist–Leninist doctrine. Communism is a broader family label, not a substitute for this period-specific description.

- [Internal Workings of the Soviet Union](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/archives/intn.html) · Library of Congress
- [Constitution of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1936/12/05.htm) · Marxists Internet Archive (USSR primary text)

### Russian Empire

1906–1914. After 1906 the Fundamental Laws combined a legislature with extensive imperial executive powers and Orthodox establishment. The state contained competing movements; nationalist conservatism alone does not describe its dynastic institutions.

- [1906 Fundamental Laws](https://novaonline.nvcc.edu/eli/evans/HIS241/Documents/RobBeaDocs/FundamentalLaws.html) · Northern Virginia Community College (Russian primary text)
- [Fundamental Laws of 1906](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/fundamental-laws-1906) · Encyclopedia of Russian History / Gale

### Republic of Venice

1718–1797. Late Venice was an oligarchic republic governed by a hereditary patriciate, with merchant interests and a restricted political community. Traditional conservatism is a modern comparison, not an official Venetian party doctrine.

- [Venice in the Eighteenth Century](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/venice-in-the-eighteenth-century) · Metropolitan Museum of Art
- [Reform, Revolution, and the End of the Republic](https://academic.oup.com/book/55872/chapter-abstract/439227989) · Oxford University Press

### Ottoman Empire

1839–1876. The 1839–1876 reform period pursued administrative centralisation, new civil institutions and qualified equality while retaining the sultanate and Islamic institutions. A capitalist-authoritarian label omits these dynastic and reforming foundations.

- [Tanzimat](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/tanzimat) · TDV Islam Encyclopaedia
- [Ottoman reform and European imperialism, 1839–1907](https://psi427.cankaya.edu.tr/uploads/files/Beinin%2C%20Ott%20Reform%20and%20European%20Imperialism.pdf) · Cambridge University Press (Joel Beinin; hosted by Cankaya University)

### Austria-Hungary

1867–1914. The dual monarchy combined hereditary imperial authority with separate Austrian and Hungarian constitutional institutions and competing national movements. It did not have one uniform nationalist party ideology.

- [Language and nationality policies](https://ww1.habsburger.net/en/stories/language-and-nationality-policies) · Schloss Schonbrunn / Habsburger.net
- [Austro-Hungarian Relations 1867–1914: A Community of Conflict](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/austrian-history-yearbook/article/austrohungarian-relations-18671914-the-perfect-example-of-a-community-of-conflict/00B04DF23DCA0B1AC0B22794EF2BD650) · Cambridge University Press

### German Empire

1871–1914. The Kaiser appointed the government within a federal constitutional monarchy containing several political parties. Conservative imperial institutions and nationalism were important, without making every citizen or parliamentary party conservative.

- [The Empire (1871–1918)](https://www.bundestag.de/en/parliament/history/parliamentarism/empire) · German Bundestag
- [Das Kaiserreich](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/kaiserreich) · Deutsches Historisches Museum

### Japanese Empire

1931–1945. During 1931–1945 military influence, imperial expansion and kokutai doctrine centred the emperor and Japanese national polity. The regime was not Nazi Germany; its relationship to the wider category of fascism is debated.

- [Selections from Kokutai no hongi (1937)](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/ps/ps_japan-kokutai.htm) · Columbia University
- [Japan: Key points in modern history](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/kpct/ct_japan.htm) · Columbia University
- [The Japanese Economy during the Interwar Period](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/research/wps_rev/rev_2009/rev09e02.htm) · Bank of Japan

### Qing China

1861–1911. The late Qing retained imperial and Confucian legitimacy while adopting selective military, industrial and constitutional reforms. Traditional conservatism is an analogy rather than a historically named governing party ideology.

- [From Reform to Revolution, 1842 to 1911](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/special/china_1750_reform.htm) · Columbia University
- [The Qing State’s Influence on Early Manufacturing of the 1910s](https://files.ehs.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/29060724/Ma-full-paper-2021.pdf) · Economic History Society

### Dutch Republic

1650–1702. Merchant regents, provincial institutions and the House of Orange competed within a confederated republic. Neoconservatism is a twentieth-century movement and is anachronistic as the republic’s classification.

- [Trouble in paradise: The Dutch golden age](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/philosophy/trouble-paradise-the-dutch-golden-age) · The Open University
- [A world power (1650–1713)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/dutch-republic-in-the-seventeenth-century/world-power-16501713/5DBB227E7E40247A588922F90DE9CCCA) · Cambridge University Press

### First French Empire

1804–1814 (before abdication). Napoleon’s hereditary imperial regime combined personal authority, plebiscitary legitimacy, civil law and revolutionary legacies. Bonapartism describes this political tradition and its later development; fascism emerged more than a century afterward.

- [Was the Napoleonic regime a military dictatorship?](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/was-the-napoleonic-regime-a-military-dictatorship/) · Fondation Napoléon
- [Consulat et Premier Empire](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/consulat-et-premier-empire) · French National Assembly
- [Le Code Civil: naissance, principes et posterite](https://www.napoleon.org/enseignants/documents/le-code-civil-21-mars-1804-naissance-principes-et-posterite/) · Fondation Napoleon

### Weimar Germany

1919–January 1933. The republic’s constitutional framework allowed competitive parties, adult suffrage and parliamentary government. Presidential emergency government increasingly weakened it after 1930. Communitarianism is a numerical comparison, not the republic’s governing doctrine.

- [The Weimar Constitution](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/weimar-germany-1918-1933/the-weimar-constitution-august-11-1919) · German Historical Institute
- [The Weimar Republic](https://www.bundestag.de/en/parliament/history/parliamentarism/weimar) · German Bundestag

### Fascist Italy

1925–1943. Mussolini’s National Fascist Party governed through dictatorship, corporatism and nationalist mobilisation while retaining the monarchy until 1943. Fascism is the period’s actual party ideology.

- [Fascismo: historical institutions and regime](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/fascismo_%28Dizionario-di-Storia%29/) · Treccani
- [Mussolini: The Doctrine of Fascism](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/mod/mussolini-fascism.asp) · Internet Modern History Sourcebook / Fordham University

### Francoist Spain

1939–1959 (before the Stabilisation Plan). Early Francoism joined military dictatorship, Catholic traditionalism, nationalism and Falangist elements. Its fascist components do not erase monarchist, clerical and military traditions or make the whole selected period identical to Italian fascism.

- [Spain: a country study](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/spaincountrystud00sols_0/spaincountrystud00sols_0.pdf) · Library of Congress
- [Spain Today: history](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/Documents/spain_today_1172013.pdf) · Government of Spain / La Moncloa

### Estado Novo Portugal

1933–1968. Salazar’s Estado Novo combined corporatism, conservative Catholic alignment, dictatorship and colonial nationalism. Fascist influence and parallels require qualification; its classification as fascism is disputed.

- [O Estado Novo: Constituicao e reformas da Administracao](https://200anos.justica.gov.pt/o-estado-novo/o-estado-novo-a-constituicao-e-as-reformas-da-administracao/) · Portuguese Ministry of Justice
- [As obras do Estado Novo](https://ensina.rtp.pt/explicador/as-obras-do-estado-novo/) · RTP

### East Germany

1971–1989 (before October). The SED’s official Marxist–Leninist doctrine and Soviet-aligned party state governed during the Honecker period. Maoism is a distinct Chinese revolutionary tradition and is not the GDR’s state ideology.

- [Marxismus-Leninismus in the GDR](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/lange-wege-der-deutschen-einheit/501199/marxismus-leninismus/) · Federal Agency for Civic Education (bpb)
- [Der Schein der Normalitat (1971 bis 1982)](https://www.bpb.de/shop/zeitschriften/izpb/geschichte-der-ddr-312/48547/der-schein-der-normalitaet-1971-bis-1982/) · Federal Agency for Civic Education (bpb)
- [What was the State Security?](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/en/stasi-records-archive/education/what-was-the-state-security/) · German Federal Archives

### Yugoslavia

1963–1980. Tito’s Yugoslavia combined communist party leadership, social ownership, worker self-management, federal decentralisation and nonalignment. Cuban Socialism is a different national tradition, despite overlapping axis scores.

- [Yugoslavia: an intelligence appraisal (1971)](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp79r01095a001300010009-8) · US Central Intelligence Agency
- [Yugoslavia: Twenty Years of Self-Management](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp79-00927a007400070002-4) · US Central Intelligence Agency
- [Constitution of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://www.cnj.it/documentazione/Cost63.htm) · Coordinamento Nazionale per la Jugoslavia (Yugoslav primary text)

### Czechoslovakia (First Republic)

1920–1938 (before October). The 1920 constitution established a democratic republic with competitive parties, rights and parliamentary institutions. The framework contained several ideologies; communitarianism was not a single official state doctrine.

- [The 1920 Constitution](https://vlada.gov.cz/scripts/detail.php?id=68721&tmplid=51) · Government of the Czech Republic
- [The First Czechoslovak Republic (1920–1938)](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~ssfc0073/Writings%20pdf/Capoccia_EEPS_2002.pdf) · Giovanni Capoccia / Oxford University

## Review limits

Political frameworks and party doctrines are not interchangeable. Parliamentary republics contained competing parties, empires contained varied territories and early Francoism/Salazarism require scholarly qualification. The Napoleonic Empire cannot be called fascist simply because a numerical neighbour is Fascism. The new labels are descriptive historical syntheses rather than a claim of universal contemporary terminology.

No answer was tuned to obtain a desired neighbour. No new ideology profile was fabricated. Original revision-1 country answers remain the permanent assessments. Full re-audits would require separate question-level work; this review does not claim all underlying educated assumptions are independently confirmed.
