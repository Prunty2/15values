# Central vs Local

Status: Draft.

Statements are listed in proposed inclusion-priority order.

1. States and regions should have a protected right to govern their own affairs, even when the national government disagrees with their policies.
2. The national government should have the final say when regional and national governments disagree over who has authority to make a decision.
3. A state or region should be allowed to leave a country or federal union if a majority of its voters supports leaving in a referendum.
4. The national government should be able to overturn a regional policy that obstructs a nationwide programme, even if the policy has regional voter support.
5. States and regions should be able to adopt their own constitutions defining how their governments work, within the rights protected by the national constitution.
6. Police forces should be directed by a single national authority rather than by separate state or regional governments.
7. State and local governments should keep most of the tax revenue collected in their area rather than send it to the national government to allocate.
8. The national government should decide what all school pupils must study, even if regions want different compulsory subjects.
9. Regional governments should be able to raise or lower their own tax rates, even if this means people pay different rates in different parts of the country.
10. Employment laws should be set nationally, even if states or regions want different rules on working hours or dismissal.
11. Local voters should be able to change the powers and boundaries of their councils without national approval.
12. The national government should decide which medical treatments public healthcare covers, even if regional governments would cover different treatments.
13. Indigenous communities should be able to govern local affairs within their territories through their own institutions rather than through national authorities.
14. The national government should set limits on how states and regions can compete with each other through lower taxes.
15. Regional governments should decide how to manage land and wildlife when the effects of those decisions stay within their region.
16. During a disaster affecting several regions, the national government should direct the emergency response, even if regional authorities prefer their own plans.

## Agreement direction

- Central: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16.
- Local: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15.
