# Political Axes

This document records the current agreed draft of 15 axes.

The axis names use simple language. Their descriptions define
what the quiz is intended to measure.

## 1. Democracy vs Autocracy

Elected, accountable government versus concentrated rule.

## 2. Authority vs Liberty

State control, order, and security versus personal freedom
and privacy.

## 3. Assimilation vs Multiculturalism

A shared national culture versus accommodating distinct
cultural identities.

## 4. Restricted Immigration vs Open Immigration

Tighter immigration limits versus easier entry and settlement.

## 5. Militarist vs Pacifist

Reliance on military strength and force versus diplomacy
and avoiding armed conflict.

## 6. Nationalism vs Internationalism

Prioritising national sovereignty versus sharing authority
through international institutions.

## 7. Public vs Private

Government ownership versus private ownership of businesses
and services.

## 8. Protectionism vs Free Trade

Protecting domestic industries versus reducing barriers
to international trade.

## 9. Planning vs Free Market

Government direction of the economy versus decisions through
markets and competition.

## 10. High Redistribution vs Low Redistribution

More redistribution through taxes and benefits versus letting
people retain more of their income and wealth.

## 11. Secular vs Religious

Keeping religion separate from government versus giving it
a role in law and public institutions.

## 12. Progressive vs Traditionalist

Changing social norms and institutions versus preserving
established traditions.

## 13. Innovation vs Caution

Prioritising technological progress and development versus
taking a cautious approach to protect people, biological
systems, and the environment.

## 14. Central vs Local

Decisions made by central government versus regional
and local institutions.

## 15. Culture vs Nature

Seeing human traits and behaviour as shaped mainly by society
and upbringing versus biology and inherited characteristics.

Here, "culture" means social influences and upbringing.
"Nature" means biological and inherited influences.

## Interpretation Notes

- The axes represent tendencies, not complete ideological packages.
- People can hold conditional or mixed positions on an axis.
- Assimilation and immigration openness are separate questions.
- National sovereignty and willingness to use military force
  are separate questions.
- Public ownership, economic planning, and redistribution
  should be distinguished when writing questions.
- Secular vs Religious measures religion's political role,
  not whether the respondent personally believes in a religion.
- Culture vs Nature should cover several kinds of human traits;
  respondents may favour different explanations in different domains.
- Innovation vs Caution includes the development and environmental
  preservation trade-offs discussed during planning.

Question wording and scoring must respect these distinctions.
